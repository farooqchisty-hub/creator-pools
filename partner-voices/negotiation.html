2. The persuasion ladder

Today a quote above twice the ceiling gets one re-measure and then a decline. That is right when the gap is unbridgeable and wrong when it is a first conversation with somebody who has never heard our side. What is missing is not more haggling, it is new information, offered in a fixed order a fixed number of times.

The three moves, each used once

The rule that makes this safe: a move may only be sent if it carries a lever not used before in this thread. Repeating "would you reconsider" in three different sentences is what makes outreach feel like pressure, and it is exactly what a model will do unless it is forbidden.

#LeverWhat it saysMoney
1Pilot framingA first collaboration, deliberately small, so both sides can see whether it works. The budget is what it is because nothing has been proven yet.no new figure
2The repeatWe run this every quarter. Somebody who does one well is on the shortlist for the next, at a rate set by what the first one did.no new figure
3Best and final, plus non-cashOur actual maximum, named once, alongside what we can add that is not money: paid amplification behind the post, a case study, partner directory placement.the ceiling

When it stops, and it must stop

  • They ask us to stop. Any opt-out language, immediate and unconditional. Acknowledge, thank, close, suppress.
  • They repeat the same number twice. A number restated is a position, not a negotiation. Close.
  • Three moves are spent. Close.
  • Their floor is still above our ceiling after move 3. Close.
  • The gap is beyond reach before we start. If their number is more than 3x our ceiling, no lever closes it and three emails is three emails of nobody's time well spent. Decline at once, warmly. Daniel James asked $5,000 against $650. Nothing on this page would have won that.

The close is a real message, not a silence. It thanks them, says plainly that the budget is what it is this quarter, and says we will come back when it moves. That last part has to be true: it means keeping them on the list rather than suppressing them, and actually returning.

3. The Klaviyo premium

Your argument is sound and worth stating precisely, because it is what separates this from paying more to whoever pushes hardest: a Klaviyo partner recommending Brevo carries weight a neutral party cannot. They are certified by the competitor. The endorsement costs them something, and that is exactly what makes it worth something.

The rule

if bucket is klaviyo_partner or klaviyo_poster
   and their floor > our ceiling
   and their floor <= 2.5 x our ceiling        <- the bound that stops it being theatre
then
   allowed = ceiling + (their_floor - ceiling) / 2      (meet halfway)
   allowed = min(allowed, PROGRAM_CEILING 1000)

The 2.5x bound is doing real work. Without it, Ben Walter gets an $875 offer he has already implied he will refuse, and Daniel James gets $1,000 against a $5,000 ask, which reads as not having listened. Both cost more relationship than a clean no would.

Two guardrails I would insist on.

First, never say why. The moment "we pay Klaviyo partners more" appears in an email it becomes a claim anybody can make, and it is unanswerable when a PushOwl partner asks why they got less for the same post. The premium is an internal pricing decision, defended internally.

Second, it is a ceiling, never an opening. The halfway number is what we may reach, not what we lead with. Leading at halfway hands away the entire concession before they have moved at all.

Any inbound stops the ladder, including an out-of-office, because an auto-reply proves the address is live and a human will see the thread.

My one hesitation, and it is yours to overrule. Four emails in six days to somebody who has never answered is fast for B2B. I would run day 3, 6, 9: the same three touches inside the same two-week window, roughly half the complaint risk. If you want two days, I will build two days and watch the first week closely.

5. Ghost follow-ups, after they have replied

A different situation from cold silence, and it deserves a different ladder. Somebody who replied and went quiet is usually deciding, checking with a colleague, or busy. They are not uninterested, and they have already told us so once.

TriggerTone
Nudge 13 business days after our last messageshort, adds one thing, asks nothing new
Nudge 22 days after thatshorter, explicitly last, door open
Parkafter nudge 2nothing sent, thread parked

Parked is not rejected, and the distinction matters. A parked thread stays out of the cold ladder, keeps its measured ceiling, and is eligible for a deliberate re-approach next quarter. Marking them rejected throws away a warm contact to tidy a dashboard.

Business days, not calendar days, for both. A Friday reply nudged on Sunday is a nudge nobody reads and a small annoyance on Monday.
