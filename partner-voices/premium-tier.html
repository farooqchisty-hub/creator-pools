The premium tier

Raising the LinkedIn ceiling to $1,000 for the top of the ICP, and what has to change around it so the money lands on the right people.

Partner Voices · 16 Sep 2026 · measured on 270 enriched profiles · for Farooq to approve

The short version. Neither engagement nor follower count identifies your top 10%. Measured on the real pool, both rank the wrong people first. What works is a hard ICP gate followed by a reach score that is bounded by what someone's posts actually do. That gives 5 people at $1,000 and 14 at $600, about 7% of the pool, for $13.4k of exposure if every one of them says yes.

Orsi is the reason to build it and also the case that exposes the gap: she is top decile on fit and middle of the pack on reach. Which of those we pay for is the decision I need from you.

2. The model

Two stages, deliberately. A gate decides who is eligible for a premium at all, then a score decides how much. Mixing them into one number is how an off-topic whale buys its way in on volume.

Stage 1: the ICP gate

All six must hold. Any failure caps the person at today's $250, it does not reject them.

TestThresholdWhy
On-topic rate5 of last 10the discriminator above
Cadence12+ posts / 90da post lands in a live feed, not a ghost town
Recencyposted within 30 daysaccount is currently active
Audience size2,500+ followersfloor below which a premium cannot be justified
Engagement ratebetween 0.05% and 8%two sided: catches dead feeds and bought engagement
Engagement floor8+ reactions or 3+ commentssomebody is reading

55 of 270 pass. Every profile in both junk tables above is excluded, each for a reason you can read out loud.

Stage 2: Effective Qualified Reach

engagement_weight = median_reactions + 3 x median_comments
effective_reach   = MIN( followers x 0.15 , engagement_weight x 120 )
EQR               = effective_reach x (on_topic / 10)

Three deliberate choices:

  • Comments count triple. They are harder to fake, they carry more algorithmic weight, and they are what a real conversation looks like.
  • The MIN is the whole trick. One term caps you at plausible organic reach for your audience size, the other caps you at what your posts demonstrably achieve. A big dead account is bounded by the second. An engagement pod with 138 followers is bounded by the first.
  • Off-topic reach is multiplied away, not subtracted. Reach into an audience that does not buy ecommerce software is worth close to nothing to us.

5. Where to start negotiating

This is the part that is actually broken today, independent of the ceiling. The current ladder counters at 50% of their quote regardless of whether their quote was reasonable. For someone whose number already sits inside our ceiling that reads as not serious, and it risks a deal we want over a discount we did not need.

The counter should scale with how far their quote sits inside the band ceiling:

Applied to Orsi at P2: her $500 against a $600 ceiling is 83%, so we open at $375 and can settle anywhere up to $500. That closes.

Applied to a $2,000 quote at P3: 200% of ceiling, so we walk. No change from today, which is correct.

6. Guardrails you did not ask about

Raising a ceiling four times changes more than the number. These are the ones I would not ship without.

  1. A monthly programme budget with a hard brake. Right now nothing stops the engine spending. Twenty premium yeses in a month is $15k. I would set a monthly cap and have the engine drop everyone to standard pricing once it is spent, rather than stop sending.
  2. A human signs anything above $250. You asked for escalations to be near zero and they are. This is the one exception I would argue for: it is about 7% of the pool, it is the only irreversible money decision in the system, and a wrong $1,000 is worth interrupting you for. Everything at or below $250 stays fully autonomous.
  3. Buy reuse rights at premium prices. Today our register explicitly promises no exclusivity and usage rights are an escalation trigger, so at $250 we buy one post and nothing else. At $600 and above I would ask for the right to repurpose the post in ads, on the site and in sales decks. That is usually worth more to us than the post.
  4. A quiet window, not exclusivity. Not "never post about a competitor", which people refuse. Something narrow: no other sponsored retention-tool post within 30 days either side. Cheap to ask for, and it stops us paying $1,000 to appear directly under an Omnisend placement.
  5. Capture the quote per format. Live bug. Orsi quoted $900 video and $500 text and the engine stored 900 as her counter. It escalated for the right reason by accident. The quote must be captured against the format we are buying.
  6. A fraud check that runs at premium prices only. The incentive to fake goes up with the cheque. The engagement rate bounds in the gate catch the obvious cases. Above $600 I would also eyeball the comment authors once, because bought comments are visibly generic.
  7. Never tell anyone their tier. Internal only. "You are in our top 10%" is an invitation to renegotiate and a liability when someone compares notes.
  8. Price consistency is a real risk. These people know each other. Ben Zettler and Orsi are both in this pool. If two comparable operators get $250 and $600, the difference has to be explainable by criteria we would be happy to say out loud. The reach bands are, seniority is; "she pushed harder" is not.
  9. Payment terms do not change. One payout, after the post is live and approved, via Tolt, 3 to 5 business days. No advance and no split at any price. Your rule, and a bigger cheque is an argument for it rather than against it.
  10. Ask for the post analytics. At $1,000 we should know what we bought. A screenshot of the post's impressions a week after publishing, as part of the deal rather than a favour.
  11. EU invoicing. Orsi is in Amsterdam and several others are EU based. At $500 plus, VAT and invoice handling stop being rounding errors. Worth a word with finance before the first one lands.
